Why it works, and its limits

  • Under MMP, moving party votes between friendly parties normally changes nothing. Seats follow party vote, so a vote moved from National to ACT just moves a seat from National to ACT.
  • The exception is overhang. If National wins more electorates than its party vote entitles it to, it keeps them all. From that point, every party vote moved to ACT or NZ First earns them seats while National loses none. Parliament grows past 120.
  • The majority bar rises too. Each extra seat enlarges Parliament, so the majority needed goes up by roughly one for every two overhang seats.
  • National's list disappears. Once National is fully in overhang, it has no list MPs. List-only MPs such as Nicola Willis and Paul Goldsmith would not return.
  • The left has overhang too. Te Pāti Māori is polling about 1% but is expected to win several Māori electorates, which already adds overhang seats on the left.
  • Seats use the Sainte-Laguë method over 120 seats. Parties need 5% or an electorate. Party votes are the nzpolls.co.nz weighted average of 14 polls since 1 September 2026.
